3 · What would have been simpler with one agent (or plain tools)

  • Tickets 101 and 102 are basically straight-line lookups. One agent with read access to all 13 tools could do 101 as get_ticket → check_stock → get_invoice → list_payments → get_cash_balance → draft, which is about 6 tool calls instead of 24. It could do 102 as get_ticket → get_lease → list_payments → get_cash_balance, about 4 calls instead of 25, with no ping-pong and no refused delegation. I'd estimate roughly a third of the ~142k tokens.
  • Some of it doesn't need an LLM at all. "Does the rent email match lease 1?" and "is $2,400 ≤ $2,560?" are deterministic checks, and so are "is invoice 501 open, and is it linked to this order?" and "8 < 20". A few lines of code (or a single MCP tool like check_rent_notice(lease_id)) would be faster, free, and never wrong. Even our cash math on the Cash tab had to be verified by code against cash_accounts, not trusted from agent text.
  • Why we still kept a team: the split is what made the safety work. Least privilege: CustomerService never saw the cash balance or list_payments, so it couldn't leak "our account is down to $160". The audit trail shows which role found each fact. The Boss has a single owner of ticket status. One agent with every tool is simpler but is also one prompt-injection away from the whole shop. A multi-agent team is worth it for messy, multi-step cases like 103 (stock + pricing + a customer reply), and overkill for 102.

4 · Three new problems this team could handle with the tools it has

  1. Restock the Crest Mug. CC-MUG-CREST has been at 0 since the start, with no invoice or ticket. Inventory finds it with check_stock/list_stock_by_size and list_vendors names Elm City Gifts (mugs, 3-day lead time). Accounting prices it with get_pricing ($3.50 cost, $14 list) and checks get_cash_balance. With only $160 left after Problem 9, a 24-mug order (24 × $3.50 = $84) fits, so the human approves a purchase through the approve_payment(kind="purchase", ref_id=2) path. We built that path but never used it.
  2. Next month's rent, or a scam version of it. Paying 102 moved lease 1's next_due to 2026-10-02. If an email arrives claiming "$2,600 due now, new bank account", Facilities compares it field by field with get_lease and flags the wrong amount and the new account as possible fraud. Accounting adds that even the real $2,400 is more than the $160 in checking. The ticket goes to waiting_on_human, and nobody pays a scammer.
  3. A duplicate invoice reminder from Bulldog Print Co. If the vendor re-sends "invoice 501 past due", Accounting runs list_payments(kind="invoice", ref_id=501) and finds payment #1, $840, approved by Shop Owner, which is the same row shown on the Cash tab. get_invoice(501) now says paid, and approve_payment would refuse a second payment anyway. The customer side (tee size S for Tauhid) moves forward with a "restock is paid, about 5 days" update.

5 · Three problems we could not solve here, and what we'd need

  1. Actually fulfilling an order, and earning money. After 101 the reprint is paid for, but no tool can receive stock (inventory never goes up), create an order, or record a sale. The Cash tab only ever goes down ($3,400 → $160) because the shop has no way to book income. We'd need a Fulfillment agent with receive_shipment(sku, size, qty), create_order, record_sale (cash in), plus an orders table and a courier tool for QuickShip CT, which sits in vendors unused.
  2. Talking to the outside world. Both customer drafts (Tauhid on 101, the Yale AI Club on 103) were approved but never sent: there is no email or SMS tool. Facilities' advice on 102, "confirm with the landlord using contact details on file", can't be followed because there's no contacts table. Tickets don't arrive on their own either; ours were seeded. We'd need a Comms agent with send_email (human-approved, logged), a contacts directory, and an inbox reader that turns new emails into tickets.
  3. Cash planning and real pricing. We ended at $160, with $2,400 rent due 10/02 and no idea whether sales will cover it. Nothing in the data shows revenue, sales history or credit. The shop clock is frozen too: desk.date_today is always 2026-08-31. And 103 showed the agents can't set a bulk price because there's no pricing policy. We'd need a Finance/Planning agent with sales history, a forecast_cash(days) tool, a payment scheduler, a bank or credit feed, and a pricing_policy table with bulk tiers and approval limits (e.g. "up to 10% off for 20+ units, owner approves more"). That would let 103 end in a real offer instead of a decline.
